## 2. Numbered Findings (Ordered by Severity)

### Finding 1: Fatal Dimensional Error and Physical Impossibility in C.2 Pre-Release Scenario (1.7–7.4 m Drop)
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Evidence:**
  - Script: `analysis/goal6_prerelease_scenario.py`, lines 49–56:
    ```python
    for extra in [40, 60, 80, 100]:
        actual_avg = rel[i15 - 2:i15 + 3].mean()
        moved = (extra - actual_avg) * 4
        q_new = max(c2v[peak_idx] - moved, 0)
        lv_new = coef[0] + coef[1] * q_new
        drop = lv[peak_idx] - lv_new
    ```
  - Artifact: `analysis/goal6/prerelease_scenario.json` (`peak_q_new`: 0 at 100 MCM/day; `drop_m`: 7.37 m)
  - Report 3 Draft: `report/ระลอกปลายกย2569_6ลุ่ม.html`, lines 343–347 (`report/goal6_build_report3_draft.py`, lines 341–347):
    *"ถ้าเพิ่มการปล่อยเป็น 40 ล้าน ลบ.ม./วัน ... ระดับสูงสุดที่ C.2 ลด ~1.7 ม. · ถ้าปล่อย 60 → ลด ~4.5 ม. · ถ้า 80 → ลด ~7 ม. — ดังนั้นปล่อยล่วงหน้าช่วยได้จริงและช่วยได้มาก"*
- **Root Cause & Flaw:**
  1. **Dimensional Incoherence:** The script calculates `moved` as a 4-day cumulative volume $\Delta V = (\text{extra} - \text{actual}) \times 4\text{ days}$ in MCM. It then directly subtracts this cumulative volume $\Delta V$ (e.g., 300 MCM) from the single-day instantaneous discharge rate $Q_{\text{peak}}$ at C.2 on October 1st (225 MCM/day): `q_new = max(c2v[peak_idx] - moved, 0)`. Subtracting volume ($L^3$) from volumetric flow rate ($L^3/T$) is a fundamental dimensional error.
  2. **Physical Impossibility:** Because $300\text{ MCM} > 225\text{ MCM/day}$, `q_new` becomes 0. The rating regression $H = 16.732 + 0.034241 \times Q$ extrapolates to $Q=0$, yielding $H = 16.73\text{ m}$ (a 7.37 m drop). The draft literally claims that pre-releasing water 15 days earlier reduces the entire Chao Phraya River discharge at Nakhon Sawan on October 1st to ZERO!
  3. **Temporal Disconnect & Self-Contradiction:** Bhumibol and Sirikit dams are 250–300 km upstream of C.2 (~3–5 days travel time). Water released on September 11–15 passes C.2 around September 14–19 and empties into the Gulf of Thailand well before October 1st. Pre-releasing water 15 days earlier cannot create a vacuum that swallows runoff generated by heavy rainfall on September 25–30 in the lower basin.
  4. **Direct Conflict with Attribution:** In the very same report (lines 304–309), the authors demonstrated that at the peak (Oct 1), the dams released only 7 MCM/day (3% of C.2 flow). If the dams' total contribution was only 7 MCM/day, even shutting the dams down entirely could reduce C.2 by at most 7 MCM/day (~0.24 m). Claiming a reduction of 1.7 to 7.4 m is off by an order of magnitude (>30x larger than total dam outflow).
- **Proposed Fix:** Retract the entire C.2 pre-release scenario from Report 3 (lines 341–358) and `prerelease_scenario.json`. Explicitly state that because upstream dams accounted for only 3% of peak discharge at C.2, pre-release operations at Bhumibol/Sirikit have negligible capacity (<0.25 m) to suppress peak downstream flood levels driven by local intermediate basin rainfall.

### Finding 3: Defective EVENT/SUSPECT Screening Rule (False Positives in In-Bank Flow, False Negatives in Catastrophic Floods)
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Evidence:**
  - Script: `analysis/goal6_screen_findings.py`, line 25:
    ```python
    x["class"] = "SUSPECT" if (h == n and r == n) else "EVENT"
    ```
  - Report Table: `analysis/goal6_screen_2026_findings.md`, Tables 2 & 3:
    - **Classed as EVENT:** B.10 (ตลาดท่ายาง, Phetchaburi): `hours_over_crit`: 291 h, `peak`: 9.44 m, `min_bank`: 13.90 m $\rightarrow$ **`max_vs_minbank` = -4.46 m**.
    - **Classed as SUSPECT:** K.10 (บ้านลุ่มสุ่ม, Mae Klong): `crit`: 10.3 m, `peak`: 47.15 m, `min_bank`: 40.70 m $\rightarrow$ **`max_vs_minbank` = +6.45 m**.
    - **Classed as SUSPECT:** K.37 (บ้านวังเย็น, Mae Klong): `peak`: 31.87 m, `min_bank`: 31.45 m $\rightarrow$ **`max_vs_minbank` = +0.42 m**.
    - **Classed as SUSPECT:** P.17 (บ้านท่างิ้ว, Ping): `peak`: 38.39 m, `min_bank`: 38.25 m $\rightarrow$ **`max_vs_minbank` = +0.14 m**.
- **Root Cause & Flaw:**
  1. The classification rule `(h == n and r == n)` tests only whether the gauge exceeded the API `critical_level` for 100% of the 2,316-hour window. It does NOT evaluate actual bank overtopping or datum offset.
  2. **Severe False Positive:** Station B.10 stayed above an arbitrary API threshold for 291 hours, so $h \ne n$. It was labeled `EVENT`, even though peak water level was **4.46 meters below the riverbank** (zero flooding, completely contained within channel).
  3. **Severe False Negative:** Station K.10 recorded water levels in meters MSL (~30–47 m.MSL), while the API datum held an unadjusted gauge height of 10.3 m. Because $47 > 10.3$ continuously, it was flagged as `SUSPECT` and discarded from the EVENT list, despite experiencing a **catastrophic overbank flood of +6.45 meters above the bank**.
- **Proposed Fix:** Redefine the screening criteria:
  1. Base inundation status directly on physical channel capacity: $\Delta h_{\text{bank}} = h_{\text{max}} - h_{\text{min\_bank}} > 0$.
  2. Flag as `DATUM_SUSPECT` whenever the API `critical_level` is inconsistent with the series range (e.g., $h_{\text{crit}} < h_{\text{min}}$ or $h_{\text{crit}} \ll h_{\text{bank}}$).
  3. Reclassify K.10, K.37, and P.17 as confirmed physical overbank flood events, and remove B.10 from the EVENT flood list.

### Finding 4: Spatial Pseudo-Replication in Headline Rainfall Metric ("67 of 199 Stations")
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Evidence:**
  - Headline claim in Brief §2, `HANDOFF.md`, and Report 3 (line 63–64, 257–258):
    *"ฝน 7 วัน 2569 ติดอันดับ 1–3 จาก 46 ปี ที่ 67/199 จุดทั่วประเทศ"*

    - Total stations evaluated: **199**
    - Unique NASA POWER 0.5° grid cells: **99**

  - Clustering breakdown by basin:
    - Bang Pakong: **10 stations occupy only 3 grid cells** (all 10 rank top-3, but represent only 3 cells).
    - Mae Klong: **10 stations occupy only 4 grid cells** (all 10 rank top-3, representing only 4 cells).
    - Chao Phraya: **9 stations occupy only 4 grid cells** (all 9 rank top-3, representing only 4 cells).
    - Tha Chin: **5 stations occupy only 2 grid cells** (all 5 rank top-3, representing only 2 cells).
- **Root Cause & Flaw:**

  2. Explicitly note in Report 3 §1 and §5 that station density in Central plain floodways concentrates station counts in storm tracks.
  3. Clarify that $(46+1)/1 = 47.0$ years is an empirical sample ranking bound, not an unbounded parametric return period.

### Finding 6: Future Information Leakage in L3 Rainfall Feature Formulation
- **Severity:** **MED**
- **Evidence:**
  - Script: `analysis/goal6_l3_model.py`, lines 92–103:
    ```python
    def rain_at(daily: dict[str, float], t: dt.datetime) -> ...:
        """R24/R72/R168 สะสมจบที่ t (ฝนวันนี้รวมชั่วโมง t ถือวัน t) ..."""
        days = []
        for k in range(7):
            d = (t - dt.timedelta(days=k)).strftime("%Y-%m-%d")
            v = daily.get(d)
            days.append(v if isinstance(v, (int, float)) else np.nan)
        r24 = days[0] if days[0] == days[0] else np.nan
    ```
- **Root Cause & Flaw:**
  - In `test_core.py` (lines 42–67), the project strictly enforces that rolling rainfall features must not look ahead (`test_day_window_uses_no_future_information`).
  - However, in `goal6_l3_model.py`, line 96 defines `days[0]` as `daily.get(d)` where `d = t.strftime("%Y-%m-%d")`.
  - At timestep $t = \text{02:00 AM}$ on day $D$, `daily.get(d)` extracts the total daily precipitation for all 24 hours of day $D$ (including rainfall occurring at 14:00, 18:00, and 23:00).
  - Feeding end-of-day rainfall into an early-morning forecast is an unacknowledged look-ahead data leakage.
- **Proposed Fix:** Align `goal6_l3_model.py` with `analysis/rain_window.py`: lag the daily rainfall series by 1 day (`days[0] = daily.get(t - 1 day)`) so that only rainfall accumulated up to yesterday is used as an input feature for real-time forecasting.

### Finding 7: Bangkok Flood Inundation Area (1,200 km²) Vulnerable to Attack Due to Arbitrary DEM Thresholding and Urban Radar Blindspots
- **Severity:** **MED**
- **Evidence:**
  - Script: `analysis/goal6_bkk_sea_mask.py`, lines 42–46:
    ```python
    dem = np.load(STAGE1 / f"dem_{bid}.npy")
    low_dem = dem < 1.2
    sea_mask = (permanent_water | low_dem) & lowland
    ```
  - Artifact: `analysis/goal6/bkk_lower/l2_sea_adjust.json` (raw: 1,320.8 km² $\rightarrow$ sea removed: 121.0 km² $\rightarrow$ reported: 1,199.8 km² $\approx$ 1,200 km²)
  - Report 3 Draft: `report/ระลอกปลายกย2569_6ลุ่ม.html`, line 73 & 277 (*"กทม. 1,200 ตร.กม."*)
- **Root Cause & Flaw:**
  1. **Vertical DEM Accuracy:** Copernicus DEM GLO-30 has a global vertical RMSE of $\pm 2\text{ to }4\text{ m}$. Applying a strict threshold `dem < 1.2 m` is noise-dominated.
  2. **Uncalibrated Coastal Masking:** Setting `dem < 1.2 m` without a topographic shoreline polygon automatically classifies low-lying inhabited polders in Samut Prakan and southern Bangkok (elevation 0.5–1.0 m.MSL) as "sea", removing actual floodwater in those areas.
  3. **Urban SAR Blindspot:** In dense urban Bangkok, C-band SAR backscatter exhibits corner-reflector double bounce off buildings, which INCREASES radar return during floods. Specular water detection ($\Delta VH \le -0.5\text{ dB}$, $VH \le -20\text{ dB}$) only detects standing water in open paddies and retention basins outside the dykes.
  4. Labeling the 1,200 km² figure as "น้ำท่วม กทม." (Bangkok flood) will be immediately attacked by BMA engineers as conflating suburban retention floodways with the Bangkok administrative metropolis.
- **Proposed Fix:**
  1. Clarify the geographic label in Report 3: change "กทม. 1,200 ตร.กม." to "พื้นที่ลุ่มเจ้าพระยาตอนล่างและทุ่งรับน้ำรอบนอก กทม. 1,200 ตร.กม.".
  2. Replace `dem < 1.2 m` with an official shoreline polygon (from OpenStreetMap or HydroSHEDS coastline).
  3. Add an explicit caveat stating that satellite SAR change detection cannot detect urban street ponding in high-density building zones.

### Finding 8: High Vulnerability of Canonical Water Rule to Harvested Rice Fields and Managed Aquaculture
- **Severity:** **MED**
- **Evidence:**
  - Script: `analysis/s1_change_detect.py`, line 41 (`DROP_VH = 0.5`, `DROP_VV = 1.5`, `ABS_VH = -20.0`, lowland < 60 m)
  - Report 3 Draft: `report/ระลอกปลายกย2569_6ลุ่ม.html`, Table L2
- **Root Cause & Flaw:**
  1. In the Central and Northern plains (e.g. Ping 2,264 km², Bang Pakong 1,282 km²), late September coincides with the harvest of the main-season wet rice crop and subsequent field preparation.
  2. Dense standing rice canopy exhibits high volume backscatter (VH ~ -12 to -15 dB). When harvested and flattened to bare smooth soil, backscatter plunges by 5–10 dB, dropping below -20 dB due to specular reflection.
  3. If farmers flood the harvested fields for stubble decomposition or off-season cultivation (ขังน้ำเตรียมแปลง), the radar signature is identical to flood inundation under all three canonical conditions.
  4. At a 2,264 km² basin scale, post-harvest paddies and controlled aquaculture constitute a significant source of false-positive flood extent that has not been separated from uncontrolled river inundation.
- **Proposed Fix:** Incorporate ESA WorldCover / land cover masks to differentiate agricultural paddies from natural floodplains, and note in the methodology that reported inundated areas include controlled agricultural flood-retention fields (ทุ่งรับน้ำ/แปลงนาเตรียมดิน).

### Finding 11: Technical Jargon, Incorrect Radar Terminology, and Text Corruption in Thai Drafts
- **Severity:** **LOW**
- **Evidence:**
  - **Radar Polarization Mistranslation:** `report/goal6_build_report3_draft.py`, line 206:
    *"สัญญาณเรดาร์ต้องลดลงอย่างน้อย 0.5 หน่วย (แนวตั้ง) และ 1.5 หน่วย (แนวนอน)"*

  - **Undefined Acronyms:** `RMSE`, `MAE`, `GBDT`, `R²`, and scenario codes `M2/M3`, `R1/M1` appear without gloss or explanation for general readers.
